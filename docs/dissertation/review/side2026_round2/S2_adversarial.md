## 1. The Case For Rejecting The Rewrite

I would argue this at the PC meeting, and I would expect to lose the vote on the benchmark and win it on the paper.

The authors were told their fix did not work. They responded by promoting the failure to a headline, adopting the vocabulary of clinical external validation, and adding a section in which they publish two bugs they found in their own pipeline. The result reads as a chastened, careful paper. It is not. Three things happened in the rewrite that a PC should not accept.

**First, the only positive result the paper still claims is an averaging artifact, and I can show it in one line.** The abstract says the percentile transform "restores calibration-in-the-large almost exactly (−0.009)". That −0.009 is the mean of a quantity that takes both signs. Per cross-institution pair, mean |calibration-in-the-large| goes from 0.301 raw to 0.249 under the transform — a 17 % reduction — and 78 % of the 2,912 pairs still miscalibrate by more than 10 percentage points, against 82 % raw *(recomputed)*. Disaggregated by target institution the transform does not repair anything: UKZN +0.440 → +0.304, Zambia −0.232 → −0.299 (worse), OULAD −0.073 → −0.174 (2.4× worse), Oviedo +0.065 → −0.084 (sign flip, no improvement in magnitude) *(recomputed)*. The near-zero grand mean is UKZN's large positive bias cancelling OULAD's and Zambia's negative bias. Nothing was restored; the errors were averaged.

**Second, the borrowed vocabulary is load-bearing and it is wrong.** The paper says its transform "is what the clinical updating ladder [13] would attempt first" and, in the Discussion, that "the cheapest repair in the clinical updating ladder — adjust the intercept, change nothing else — is what our percentile transform turns out to be". I ran the actual first rung. Intercept-only recalibration on the same 2,912 pairs takes the Brier score from 0.361 to **0.223** (the transform reaches 0.322), drives per-pair calibration-in-the-large to exactly zero on **every** pair (the transform leaves 78 % above 0.10), improves failing-class F1 from 0.420 to 0.432 (the transform *lowers* it to 0.348), and leaves AUC and recall-at-20 % numerically unchanged at 0.5972 and 0.2729, as a monotone map must *(recomputed)*. The paper's transform moves AUC by +0.019. An intercept adjustment cannot move AUC. The analogy is falsified by the paper's own Table in V-C. The paper claims kinship with a procedure it never ran, and the procedure beats it on every calibration metric.

**Third, the two negative results are leading because the two positive results were deleted.** `pooled.csv` and `fewshot.csv` are in the released f33 artifact and are cited nowhere in the rewrite. A model pooled over the four *other* institutions, percentile representation, zero target labels, reaches AUC 0.683 against the within-cohort 0.692 — a transfer gap of 0.009, not the 0.094 the abstract reports — with Brier 0.271 and recall-at-20 % of 0.314 *(recomputed from the released artifact)*. Add 100 labelled target students and it reaches AUC 0.712, Brier 0.196, recall 0.335 — at or above local training on all three *(ibid.)*. In v1 the few-shot result was reported as a negative ("adding labelled students does not close the gap"); the round-1 panel told the authors that the negative was probably an artifact of the wrong refit strategy and listed it under *report as negative results, prominently, with the caveat*. Under the corrected metric suite the result reversed and became positive. It was not reported with the caveat. It was removed, along with the pooled section, and the paper now says "We do not propose a new algorithm" and leads with two failures. That is selective reporting in the modest direction, which is still selective reporting, and it materially changes what a reader concludes about whether cross-institution early warning is viable.

Add to this: the paper's own contamination finding (V-G) invalidates the explanation ladder it still reports in V-E, and was applied only to AUC (see §2.4); the fairness section's "local" baseline is a resubstitution fit (§2.5); the mechanism analysis in V-C still covers three of the five institutions, which round 1 explicitly required be fixed (§2.7); and the seven "canonical" features are not the same quantities across platforms, one of them being forum *posts* at KU Leuven and forum *clicks* at the Open University (§2.1).

What survives is the dataset assembly, the institution-dependence table (V-A), and the contamination audit as a measurement. That is a resource paper and a short methods note. It is not a four-contribution Track 1 paper, and the paper as written does not say so.

## 2. What The First Panel Missed

This is the section that matters. Seven items; the first five I regard as decisive.

### 2.1 The percentile transform destroys information at exactly the institutions it is supposed to help, and it does so through ties

Round 1 established (DA-3) that percentile features force each cohort's marginals to Uniform[0,1] and therefore reproduce the source's alert rate. Nobody checked what the transform does when a feature has no variance to rank.

`represent()` uses `x.rank(pct=True, method="average")`. On an all-zero column of length *n*, every student receives the identical value (n+1)/2n ≈ 0.500 — the **median** of the source's scale.

KU Leuven's `cum_social` is 99.9 % zeros. Under the transform, every KU Leuven student is presented to an OULAD-trained model as a median forum user *(recomputed)*:

| cohort | `cum_social` raw mean | % zero | percentile mean | percentile min | percentile max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ku_Accountancy_1819 | 0.001 | 99.9 % | 0.501 | 0.500 | 1.000 |
| ku_Globaleconom_1920 | 0.010 | 99.3 % | 0.501 | 0.497 | 0.999 |
| oulad_BBB_2013J | 168.4 | 26.0 % | 0.500 | 0.130 | 1.000 |
| oviedo_C4189_1415 | 0.023 | 97.7 % | 0.504 | 0.492 | 0.992 |

Share of a cohort's students sitting on the single modal percentile value, averaged within institution *(recomputed)*:

| institution | active_weeks | cum_active_days | cum_clicks | cum_content | **cum_social** | cur_clicks | weeks_since_active |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| KU Leuven | 0.679 | 0.088 | 0.028 | 0.036 | **0.954** | 0.115 | **0.885** |
| OULAD | 0.243 | 0.099 | 0.098 | 0.098 | 0.187 | 0.494 | 0.506 |
| Oviedo | 0.290 | 0.152 | 0.109 | 0.196 | 0.503 | 0.411 | 0.659 |
| UKZN | 0.303 | 0.077 | 0.027 | 0.061 | 0.431 | 0.220 | 0.783 |
| Zambia | 0.192 | 0.094 | 0.050 | 0.094 | 0.313 | 0.196 | 0.833 |

Three of KU Leuven's seven features are near-constant after the transform. Under raw counts, "this student has zero forum activity" is at least placed at the bottom of the source's scale; the transform moves it to the middle. This is why the transform makes calibration *worse* at Zambia (−0.232 → −0.299) and OULAD (−0.073 → −0.174) while improving it at UKZN. It is a defect in the paper's only proposed method and it is mechanical, not statistical.

**Underlying this: the seven features are not commensurable, and the code says so.** `run_transfer_ladder.load_ku` sets `"current_social_clicks": g["forum_posts"]` — a count of forum *post-creation* events (`DTCREATED` rows). `oulad_adapter` sets the same column to summed `sum_click` on `forumng`/`oucollaborate`/`ouelluminate`/`ouwiki` — a *click volume*. `moodle_logs.build_cohort_frame` sets it to a count of log rows with `Component ∈ {Forum, Chat}` — a *view* count. Three different quantities under one column name, differing by two to five orders of magnitude. Likewise `current_clicks`: OULAD sums `sum_click` (interactions per site per day, ≥ 1 per record); UKZN/Zambia use `grp.size()` (one per log row). Section III-C's claim that "seven leakage-safe features are derived identically" is not what the adapters do. Round 1's methodology seat verified leakage-safety of the derivations, which is a different property, and did not check semantic commensurability.

Minor rider on the same sentence: OULAD's `current_week_clicks` sums an `assessment` category (`quiz`, `externalquiz`, `questionnaire`, `dataplus`). "No demographic or assessment features enter the model" (III-C) is loose — assessment *interaction volume* is inside `cum_clicks` for OULAD and inside the Moodle log totals elsewhere.

### 2.2 The calibration result is one institution

93 % of the headline calibration collapse comes from UKZN. Per-target-institution mean calibration-in-the-large at D3, raw, gradient boosting *(recomputed)*: UKZN +0.440 (16 targets), KU Leuven +0.173 (6), Oviedo +0.065 (20), OULAD −0.073 (19), Zambia −0.232 (2). Weighting by target count, UKZN contributes +0.111 of the reported +0.119.

The reason is visible in `cohorts.csv`: UKZN pass rates run 0.735 to 0.950 — failure prevalence of 5 % to 27 % — while the source institutions sit at 0.45 (OULAD) to 0.75 (Zambia) failure. "Calibration collapses across institutions" reduces, on this benchmark, to "UKZN passes almost everybody and the other four do not". That is a base-rate difference, and it is the textbook case (Van Calster et al. 2019, *BMC Medicine* 17:230: "When an algorithm is developed in a setting with a high disease incidence, it may systematically give overestimated risk estimates when used in a setting where the incidence is lower"). Five institutions were not needed to observe it; two with different prevalence would have sufficed. The paper should report the per-institution breakdown and say plainly that its calibration finding is driven by one target institution's prevalence.

### 2.3 The clinical ladder's first rung, actually run, dominates the paper's transform

Direct test of the paper's central analogy. D3 only (2,912 ordered cross-institution pairs), gradient boosting, macro-averaged over the 63 target cohorts. `intercept_only` refits a single intercept with the source linear predictor as offset and the slope fixed at 1 — rung 1 of Vergouwe et al.'s closed-testing procedure, the rung the paper says its transform "turns out to be" *(recomputed; `scratchpad/t5.py`)*:

| representation | update | AUC | Brier | mean CIL | mean \|CIL\| | recall@20 % | F1 (fail) | share \|CIL\| > 0.10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| raw | none | 0.5972 | 0.3610 | +0.1195 | 0.3011 | 0.2729 | 0.4200 | 81.6 % |
| raw | **intercept-only** | **0.5972** | **0.2227** | 0.0000 | **0.0000** | **0.2729** | **0.4317** | **0.0 %** |
| percentile | none *(the paper's fix)* | 0.6163 | 0.3223 | −0.0087 | 0.2487 | 0.2733 | 0.3479 | 77.9 % |
| percentile | intercept-only | 0.6163 | 0.2227 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.2733 | — | 0.0 % |

Read the raw rows. AUC and recall are byte-identical before and after, as monotonicity requires — the paper's "recalibrate before you do anything else, and do not expect recalibration to make the model better at finding students" is correct *and is a property of the rung it did not run, not of the transform it did*. Brier falls by 0.138 against the transform's 0.039. Per-pair miscalibration goes to zero rather than to 0.249. Failing-class F1 goes up rather than down 0.07.

The honest framing of the percentile transform is not "this is the ladder's first rung". It is: *this is a label-free surrogate for the ladder's first rung, it recovers about a quarter of the available Brier improvement, it does not fix per-pair calibration, and it costs 0.07 of failing-class F1.* And if a label-free surrogate is the contribution, the comparator is Saerens, Latinne & Decaestecker (2002) — which the paper cites at [16], describes as "label-free prior correction has existed since", and never runs. A referee will ask for that baseline and the paper has pre-announced that it knows about it.

### 2.4 The paper's own contamination finding invalidates a claim it still makes in V-E

V-G reports that 72 of 78 D1 pairs and 324 of 916 D2 pairs share students between training and evaluation, and shows the corrected AUC (D1 0.715 → 0.656). The correction is applied to AUC only. V-E's ladder is computed on all pairs:

> "Agreement declines monotonically with distance — 0.207 at D1, 0.102 at D2, 0.026 at D3"

Restricted to the pairs the authors' own `exp_017/pair_overlap.csv` marks clean *(recomputed)*:

| distance | τ, all pairs | n | τ, clean pairs | n | J@3 all | J@3 clean |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 same course | 0.207 | 78 | **0.086** | 10 | 0.428 | 0.340 |
| D2 other course | 0.102 | 916 | 0.089 | 771 | 0.370 | 0.362 |
| D3 other institution | 0.026 | 2,912 | 0.026 | 2,909 | 0.323 | 0.323 |

The decline is no longer monotonic: D1 and D2 become indistinguishable (0.086 vs 0.089), and D1 loses 59 % of its value. Per representation, the clean D1 τ is 0.210 (raw), 0.086 (z-score) and **−0.038 (percentile — below the random null)**. Clean D1 top-3 Jaccard of 0.340 sits barely above the analytic random baseline of 0.303. The sentence quoted above is a claim about a gradient that exists only because 92 % of the D1 pairs are contaminated. Round 1 forced the authors to strike "monotonically" as a factual error in v1 (DA-misc); it is back in V-E, on contaminated data.

### 2.5 The fairness section's "local" model is fitted and scored on the same rows

`run_fairness_audit.main` builds `models[c.cohort_id]` by fitting on `c.X(rep), c.y`, then sets `sources = [("local", target)] + [("transferred", s) for s in cohorts if s.institution != target.institution]` and scores every source model on `target.X(rep)`. For `kind == "local"` that is a resubstitution prediction. The ladder's own D0 uses `cross_val_predict` with 5-fold stratification; the fairness audit does not.

The inflation is not academic *(recomputed)*:

| institution | attribute | cohorts behind the "local" figure | in-sample worst-group recall | in-sample best-group recall | **5-fold CV whole-cohort recall@20 %** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| UKZN | GENDER | 5 | 0.735 | 0.794 | 0.344 |
| UKZN | QUINTILE | **3** | 0.654 | 0.779 | 0.330 |
| UKZN | RACE | **1** | 0.526 | 0.628 | 0.303 |
| OULAD | imd_band | 17 | 0.285 | 0.467 | 0.373 |
| OULAD | gender | 19 | 0.392 | 0.427 | 0.376 |

At UKZN the in-sample "local" worst-group recall (0.654) is roughly **double** the honest cross-validated whole-cohort recall (0.330). At OULAD, where cohorts are 1,000–2,500 students, the inflation is small; at UKZN, where the failing class is 17–184 students, the model memorises it. The abstract's sentence — "at UKZN, recall for the lowest school-quintile group falls from 0.65 locally to 0.18 after transfer" — compares a resubstitution estimate over three cohorts with an out-of-sample estimate over 141 source–target pairs. Most of that fall is the train-on-test gap, not transfer. The `Race (UKZN)` row of Table V-F rests on **one cohort and one model**; "should be read as indicative" is not adequate disclosure for n = 1.

Two further problems in the same section that nobody raised:

*The "worst-served group" is not a group.* `recall_worst` is `min` over 2–6 groups per (target, source). For UKZN QUINTILE the locally worst quintile is 3, 1 and 3 across the three cohorts; after transfer the argmin is spread across all five (3:48, 2:34, 4:23, 5:19, 1:17). For OULAD `imd_band` the local argmin covers six deprivation bands and the transferred argmin covers all ten *(recomputed)*. There is no stable worst-served group; there is a noisy minimum. "Transfer harms the worst-served group most" is a statement about an order statistic, and `recall_gap = max − min` is an extreme-value statistic whose expectation grows with noise — which is precisely what distinguishes the transferred condition (47–836 noisier models) from the local one (1–19 in-sample models).

*The label-collinearity limitation is not applied here.* Section III-A states that "Label definition and institution are perfectly collinear here, so no analysis in this paper can separate a label effect from an institution effect", and VI repeats it. Every UKZN "transferred" model in V-F was trained on OULAD (withdrawal counted as failure), Oviedo (gradebook total), KU Leuven or Zambia labels. By the paper's own stated bound, V-F cannot separate "transfer harms the lowest quintile" from "a model trained to predict withdrawal, applied under a registrar-pass rule, harms the lowest quintile". The abstract states the fairness result without that bound. Either the bound applies to V-F, in which case the abstract sentence must be qualified, or it does not, in which case III-A is overstated. It cannot be both.

### 2.6 Undisclosed aggregation switching, and an inferred-calendar disclosure gap

**Aggregation.** Every discrimination and calibration number in V-B/V-C/V-D is a macro-average over target cohorts within one representation — I reproduced 0.691 / 0.597 / 0.361 / +0.119 / 0.272 / 0.421 and 0.617 / 0.322 / −0.009 / 0.274 / 0.348 exactly that way. Every explanation number is a micro-average over pairs **pooled across all three representations**. The pooled triple is (0.2072, 0.1025, 0.0261); the like-for-like raw-representation triple is (0.2241, 0.1069, 0.0319) *(recomputed)*. This matters because the paper then writes "Cross-institution agreement of 0.026 … and the percentile transform does not help (0.018)" — comparing a three-representation average against a single representation. The raw-to-percentile comparison is 0.032 → 0.016, i.e. the transform *halves* explanation agreement. Same defect in the V-H sensitivity row: "explanation agreement stays between 0.026 and 0.038" is the pooled series; the raw-representation series across the three cutoffs is 0.004 / 0.032 / 0.023, an eightfold range. "Stays between" asserts a stability the like-for-like series does not show.

**Calendars.** Round 1's methodology seat filed the inferred calendar as `[MINOR] C13` — no leakage, a deployment gap, state it. Two things it did not check:

*Some cohorts have no data at the cutoff.* `moodle_logs` infers the span as first-to-last week with ≥ 10 % of enrolled students active, and the cutoff is `rint(0.33 × n_weeks)`. `oviedo_C4189_1415` gets a 43-week span, cutoff week 14, and **82.8 % of its 128 students have zero cumulative clicks at that row**; its within-cohort AUC is 0.501. Three more Oviedo cohorts are 22–32 % dead at the cutoff (C2607 31.8 %, D0 0.451; C2335 24.3 %, D0 0.509; C3022 21.8 %, D0 0.535) *(recomputed)*. These four cohorts supply the bottom of the "0.384–0.953" Oviedo range that the paper presents in V-A as evidence that the engagement signal is institution-specific. At least part of that range is the calendar inference placing the cutoff outside the teaching period of a second-semester course inside a year-long log span.

*The same course gets different calendars in different years, and that is the D1 rung.* `ukzn_ISTN211` is inferred at 37 weeks in 2019 and 19 weeks in 2021 — cutoff week 12 against week 6. `ukzn_ISTN101`: 33/35/24 weeks, cutoffs 11/12/8. `ukzn_ISTN2IP`: 20/33, cutoffs 7/11. `zambia_ICT1110`: 43/36, cutoffs 14/12 *(recomputed)*. The "same course in another year" rung therefore sometimes compares a model trained at week 12 with an application at week 6 of the same subject.

*To the authors' credit, I tested whether this drives the result and it does not.* Institution-controlled, UKZN D1 AUC by cutoff mismatch is 0.626 (Δ=0), 0.635 (Δ=1), 0.661 (Δ=3), 0.663 (Δ=4), 0.577 (Δ=6) — no monotone relation, n ≤ 6 per cell. Across the 44 non-OULAD cohorts, Spearman(D0 AUC, inferred n_weeks) = −0.19 (p = 0.21) and Spearman(D0 AUC, share dead at cutoff) = −0.07 (p = 0.68). The strong version of the hypothesis — that inferred calendars manufacture the cross-institution gap — is **not** supported, and I record that. What remains is a disclosure failure: III-C says "Course lengths range from 14 to 46 weeks" as though that were a property of the courses. It is a property of the inference. A 43-to-45-week "course" is an academic year. And the deployment gap C13 asked for (at prediction time you do not know how long the course will run) is still not stated anywhere in the manuscript.

The current `exp_018/summary.csv` gives a noise floor of **0.707 (sd 0.244)**, which is what V-E prints. - **`exp_018` reports two ceilings and V-E reports one without saying so.** `summary.csv`: "ceiling (disjoint halves, same cohort)" τ = 0.198, J@3 = 0.415; "ceiling, ladder-matched evaluation set" τ = 0.338, J@3 = 0.487. V-E prints the second. That is the *conservative* choice for the paper's argument and I do not object to it, but the existence of the other estimate must be stated. Separately, V-E reports four point estimates with no dispersion; the ceiling's sd is 0.338, equal to its mean, and the floor's is 0.244.
- **Table/figure numbering.** The body cites "Table II" and "Fig. 2a" exactly once each and no other table or figure anywhere; none of the manuscript's eight tables is numbered and no figure exists in the file. The journal-fit seat's finding is half-addressed.

## 3. Challenge-by-challenge

### C1 — Is there a contribution left? **[CRITICAL]**

**The case that there is not.** Strip it to true-and-new and you get: (a) an assembly of five public datasets under a shared schema, which is engineering, not science, and whose value depends entirely on the schema being sound — §2.1 shows it is not; (b) "a transported risk model keeps discrimination and loses calibration", which is textbook clinical prediction modelling, settled since Steyerberg (2009) and Janssen et al. (2008 *JCE*), restated as consensus in Debray et al. (2015 *JCE*) and Van Calster et al. (2019 *BMC Medicine*) — the paper now cites the last of these, so it is confirming a known result in a new domain; (c) a proposed repair that the paper concedes does not improve the decision, and which §2.2–§2.3 show also does not repair calibration per-pair and is beaten by the standard method it claims to resemble; (d) an explanation-instability finding whose within-cohort component (τ = 0.338 against a random null of 0.000, J@3 = 0.487 against 0.303) is a restatement of Hooker, Mentch & Zhou (2021), whom the paper cites for exactly that; and (e) two "hazards" that are textbook everywhere and published in the subfield's own flagship journal (C4). On that reading the contribution is: *we assembled five datasets and confirmed four known results, one of them incorrectly.*

**The case that there is.** The confirmation framing is too cheap, for three reasons. First, "known in cardiology" and "known in learning analytics" are different epistemic states, and the *magnitude* is not known in advance: nobody could have said before this experiment that within-cohort AUC ranges 0.845 (OULAD) to 0.527 (Zambia) across public LMS datasets. Table V-A is a genuinely new empirical fact and it has real consequences for how the field reads its own literature — a single-dataset paper reporting 0.85 and one reporting 0.53 may be doing identical work. Second, the explanation result *is* new in its comparative form: round 1's domain seat concluded that gap claim (c) — nobody has compared a transferred model's explanation against a locally trained model's — survives, and I did not find a counterexample either. Third, the benchmark is a real cost that someone had to pay; five adapters over five platforms in four countries is months of work and the field does not have it.

**Which is right.** The second, but only for a paper that is honest about being a resource paper with one new empirical claim. The paper as written is not that paper: it advertises four contributions, of which #3 (the repair) is wrong as described, #4 (the hazards) is not new, and #5 (fairness) is measured against a resubstitution baseline. If those three were withdrawn or restated, the residue — a public five-institution transfer benchmark, the V-A range, the explanation comparison against a measured ceiling, and the contamination audit as a *measurement* — is a solid, publishable, modest paper. The reviewer's question is not "is anything left" but "does the paper claim what is left". It does not.

### C2 — The borrowed vocabulary **[CRITICAL]**

Three uses, one correct, one decorative, one wrong.

*Correct.* TRIPOD+AI [11] as a reporting frame and the discrimination/calibration separation are used properly and are the best thing about the rewrite. Reporting Brier and calibration-in-the-large beside AUC is exactly right.

*Decorative.* "In the vocabulary of [12] this is calibration-in-the-large restored while weaker calibration remains imperfect" (V-C). Van Calster's hierarchy is mean → weak → moderate → strong calibration; "weaker calibration" is not a level in it, and the sentence is not doing work — the paper never reports a calibration slope, which is the level the hierarchy would require next. Also a citation slip: the calibration hierarchy is Van Calster et al. 2016 (*J Clin Epidemiol* 74:167–176), not the 2019 *BMC Medicine* paper cited at [12], which restates it. [13] is dated 2016 in the reference list; it is *Stat Med* 36(28):4529–4539, 2017.

*Wrong, and this is the CRITICAL part.* "It is what the clinical updating ladder [13] would attempt first" (V-C) and "the cheapest repair in the clinical updating ladder — adjust the intercept, change nothing else — is what our percentile transform turns out to be" (VI). Rung 1 of Vergouwe et al. is a **supervised update of the model's intercept using target outcomes**. The percentile transform is an **unsupervised monotone transform of the input features**. They differ in what they consume (labels vs none), in what they touch (the link vs the covariates), and observably in what they do: an intercept update is rank-preserving and the transform is not. The paper's own V-C table shows AUC moving 0.597 → 0.617 under the transform, which is by itself a proof that it is not an intercept adjustment.

**Would a proper intercept recalibration beat it?** Yes, decisively, and the artifacts do let you test it — §2.3 has the table. On the calibration objective the rung wins on every metric and loses on none: Brier 0.223 vs 0.322, per-pair |CIL| 0.000 vs 0.249, failing-class F1 0.432 vs 0.348, AUC and recall exactly preserved.

The defensible version of the paper's claim is narrow and worth making: *the percentile transform is a label-free device that reproduces some of what intercept recalibration does when target labels are unavailable*. To make it, the paper must (i) run the supervised rung as the reference point, (ii) run Saerens et al. [16] as the label-free comparator it already cites, (iii) report per-pair |CIL| not the signed mean, and (iv) stop saying the transform *is* the ladder's first rung.

### C3 — Negative results as shield **[CRITICAL]**

Avoidance, not honesty. §1 and §2.7 give the evidence: the pooled-source and few-shot results are in the released artifacts, both are strongly positive under the corrected metric suite (pooled cross-institution AUC 0.683 vs within-cohort 0.692; few-shot at k=100 reaching 0.712 AUC / 0.196 Brier / 0.335 recall, all at or better than local training), the round-1 panel explicitly asked for the few-shot result to be reported prominently with a caveat, and both were deleted instead. A rewrite that deletes its positive results and promotes its negative ones has not become more honest; it has changed which direction its selection points.

What would a PC conclude from V-D and V-E as they stand? Neither "careful" nor "no result" — my honest prediction is *"the authors are performing carefulness"*. V-D's negative is real and well-argued. V-E's negative is real but is a restatement of a cited paper ([18]) plus a measured ceiling, and its supporting gradient (§2.4) does not survive the authors' own contamination audit. Two negatives lead the abstract; the deleted positives would have changed the paper's headline claim from "an ordinary model loses about 0.1 AUC when it crosses an institutional boundary" to "a single-source model loses 0.1 AUC; a multi-source model loses 0.01". That is not a presentational choice, it is the paper's main quantitative claim.

### C4 — The hazards section **[MAJOR]**

Neither hazard is novel. One of them is published in the target subfield's own flagship journal.

**Majority-class F1.** Bosch, N. & Paquette, L. (2018), "Metrics for Discrete Student Models: Chance Levels, Comparisons, and Use Cases", *Journal of Learning Analytics* 5(2):86–104, doi:10.18608/jla.2018.52.6, states the hazard almost verbatim: *"when the positive class was the majority, we noted that the best possible F1 differed little from chance-level F1 … F1 will not greatly exceed chance level even in a relatively accurate model"*, and *"comparing F1 to a naïve baseline without considering imbalances in data and predicted class proportions can result in an inaccurate picture of performance"*; its "Notes for Practice" recommends *"comparing F1 scores to the appropriate chance level"*. That is V-G's entire content, in *JLA*, eight years ago. The always-positive baseline specifically is the headline methodological recommendation of Flach & Kull, "Precision-Recall-Gain Curves: PR Analysis Done Right", NeurIPS 2015, §3.1: *"the baseline to beat is the always-positive classifier rather than any random classifier … any model with prec < π or rec < π loses against this baseline."* The class-swap asymmetry is formalised in Sokolova & Lapalme (2009, *IP&M* 45(4):427–437), Powers (2011), and Chicco & Jurman (2020, *BMC Genomics* 21:6), whose Use Case A1 is a 91 %-positive dataset on which a classifier catching zero negatives scores F1 = 0.95. `pos_label=1` is a documented scikit-learn default. Related LA priors: Pelánek (2015, *JEDM* 7(2)); Whitehill et al. (2017, *L@S*).

**Shared students.** The principle is canon. Kaufman, Rosset, Perlich & Stitelman (2012, *ACM TKDD* 6(4):15) calls leakage one of the top ten data-mining mistakes; Saeb et al. (2017, *GigaScience* 6(5)) shows record-wise CV holding error near 2 % where subject-wise ranges 7–27 % and urges researchers to *"avoid record-wise cross-validation"*; Roberts et al. (2017, *Ecography* 40(8):913–929) generalises to any hierarchical structure; Kapoor & Narayanan (2023, *Patterns* 4(9):100804) list it twice in their eight-type taxonomy (L1.4 duplicates, L3.2 non-independence between train and test), across 294 affected papers in 17 fields; `GroupKFold` has shipped since scikit-learn 0.18 (2016). In education specifically, student-level splitting is the standard protocol in knowledge tracing (pyKT, NeurIPS D&B 2022, motivated by *"wrong evaluation setting may cause label leakage that generally leads to performance inflation"*), and EDM has had its own duplicated-rows scandal for a decade (Xiong, Zhao, Van Inwegen & Beck, EDM 2016, on ASSISTments 2009–2010). OULAD's own descriptor documents that *"modules can be presented multiple times during the year"* and that the analytic unit is the student–module–presentation triplet.

What *is* unreported is narrower: I could find no peer-reviewed LA/EDM paper quantifying per-pair student overlap as a confound in a cross-course/cross-cohort transfer ladder. (Cross-course overlap is known in LA — it is used as a *feature*, e.g. *Electronics* 15(3):642, 2026, aligning latent spaces via students who took both courses.) So the measurement is new; the hazard is not.

**Verdict.** V-G as written invites a one-line desk rejection ("see Bosch & Paquette 2018, same journal"). It is salvageable in one of two forms, both honest: (i) *we apply established evaluation standards from ML and clinical prediction to cross-institution LA transfer and the standard ladder fails all of them*, cited to Flach & Kull, Bosch & Paquette, Saeb, Kapoor & Narayanan — a framing claim, cheap and defensible; or (ii) a compliance audit — *despite Bosch & Paquette (2018), N of M recent LA transfer papers report majority-class F1 without a baseline* — which is a real finding and would take a weekend. "The field does not know this" is not available; a single *JLA* citation falsifies it.

One rider: I could not verify a figure that circulated in my literature search ("78 % of 50 audited EDM papers, median +12pp inflation", attributed to arXiv:2511.11866). On fetching that preprint no such audit exists in it. Do not use that number.

### C5 — What the first panel missed **[CRITICAL]**

§2. Ranked by severity: (2.3) the ladder's first rung beats the transform; (2.1) the transform maps degenerate features to the median and the seven features are not commensurable across platforms; (2.4) the contamination finding invalidates V-E's gradient; (2.5) the fairness "local" baseline is resubstitution and the worst-served group is not a stable group; (2.2) the calibration collapse is one institution's base rate; (2.6) undisclosed aggregation switching and inferred-calendar disclosure; (2.7) four required round-1 fixes silently unmade.

Two hypotheses from the brief that I tested and **could not** support, recorded so the authors are not asked to chase them: (i) inferred calendars do not measurably manufacture the cross-institution AUC gap (institution-controlled D1 AUC shows no monotone relation to cutoff mismatch; across 44 non-OULAD cohorts Spearman(D0 AUC, n_weeks) = −0.19, p = 0.21); (ii) the ⅓ cutoff does land at systematically different points of the teaching cycle, and does produce four Oviedo cohorts with 22–83 % of students dead at the cutoff, but the resulting D0 AUCs (0.45–0.53) sit inside the range the paper already reports rather than driving it.

## 5. Verdict

**Reject, with an explicit invitation to resubmit as a resource-and-negative-results paper.** I would not vote major revision a second time: the round-1 revision cycle demonstrated that this group responds to a finding by reframing rather than by re-running, and four of its required fixes were silently not made. A resubmission gives the next panel a clean object to judge.

The benchmark is real and the field wants it. The V-A range (0.845 to 0.527 within-cohort AUC across five public datasets) is a genuinely new empirical fact worth a paper by itself. The explanation comparison against a measured within-cohort ceiling is, as far as I can determine, new. Those three things plus an honest calibration section would make a good six-page paper.

Non-negotiable for acceptance, in dependency order:

1. **Run the clinical updating ladder you cite.** Intercept-only, then intercept+slope, then Saerens et al. [16] as the label-free comparator. Report the percentile transform against them, and stop saying it *is* rung 1.
2. **Report per-pair \|calibration-in-the-large\| and the per-target-institution breakdown.** The signed grand mean must not appear in the abstract unaccompanied. State that UKZN's prevalence drives 93 % of the reported collapse.
3. **Recompute the fairness local arm out of sample**, drop the n = 1 row, and either report the worst-group identity's instability or replace the min statistic with something that is not an extreme value.
4. **Apply the contamination filter to V-E as well as to AUC**, and withdraw "declines monotonically".
5. **Restore or explicitly disclose the pooled and few-shot results.** A paper whose abstract says a transferred model loses 0.1 AUC, while its own released artifact shows a pooled transferred model losing 0.01, is not reporting its data.
6. **Restate V-G against Bosch & Paquette (2018), Flach & Kull (2015), Saeb et al. (2017) and Kapoor & Narayanan (2023)**, as an application of known standards or as a compliance audit — not as discovery.
7. **Fix the schema disclosure**: `social` is three different quantities, `clicks` is two, three of KU Leuven's seven features are near-constant after the percentile transform, and the cutoff rule in the text is not the cutoff rule in the code.
8. **Rerun `exp_016` over all five institutions** or state the restriction where the claim is made.
9. **Restore the leave-one-institution-out sensitivity** that round 1 upheld and the rewrite dropped, and cite Fisher, Rudin & Dominici for permutation importance.
10. **One aggregation throughout**, stated in the Methods.

Items 1–5 change what the paper claims. That is the same sentence the round-1 editor wrote, and it is still true.
